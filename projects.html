Federated learning · Active learning · Human oversight

FedSALAT Lab — learning with fewer labels

I built an interactive lab showing how federated clients decide which incoming examples are worth labelling. Three simulated clients learn from digit-image streams, train on small annotation buffers, and combine their models through federated averaging.

Visitors can act as the human annotator, introduce rotation drift, and compare adaptive-threshold, fixed-threshold, and random selection under matched label budgets. The lab makes the trade-off between annotation effort and predictive performance visible through measured metrics and threshold histories.

LLM evaluation · Human–AI judgment · Research communication

The Judgment Lab — who would you believe?

I built an interactive companion to my exploratory study of how humans and AI judge arguments. Visitors read original AI debate transcripts, choose the stronger case, and optionally rate trust, persuasion, and relevance before revealing the assigned rhetorical tones.

The underlying study includes 18 debates combining friendly, aggressive, and sycophantic tones with three debate formats, evaluated by 12 human volunteers and four AI judges. The lab presents the reported findings alongside their limitations; visitors’ new judgments stay in their browser and do not become part of the original study.

LLM-as-judge evaluation · Rhetorical tone · Local judgment tracking

Safe decision-making · Learned chess policies · Risk modelling

Kinghunter — a chess AI under the microscope

I built a playable research demo pairing a learned chess policy with a separate model trained to estimate blunder risk from Stockfish-derived labels. Its Decision Lab lets visitors compare the policy’s preferred move, minimum-risk selection, and threshold-based soft shielding.

The project makes the interaction between policy preference and predicted risk inspectable, with controls for candidate selection and risk thresholds. It reports frozen-position and full-game evaluations separately: improvements on the position benchmark do not imply a strong chess engine. An optional rule-based immediate-mate guard is identified separately from the learned shield.

Convolutional neural networks · Stockfish-derived supervision · Oracle-guided soft shielding
